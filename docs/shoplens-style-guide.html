ShopLens style guide

The reference for everything user-facing. One principle above all: the input is the product, everything else stays quiet.

Color

The palette is warm paper, dark ink, one working color, and one personality color. Blue does the functional work. Butter yellow marks exactly one thing at a time: the current selection.

Rules

Do
  • Use Blue for every primary action. One page, one working color.
  • Reserve Butter for the single active selection on screen.
  • Put Ink text on Butter. Contrast fails with white.
Do not
  • Introduce per-category colors. Lenses are differentiated by icon and label, not hue.
  • Use Butter for buttons, links, or anything that navigates.
  • Use pure black or pure gray text anywhere.

Components

Buttons

Primary is Blue, pill-shaped, sentence case, verb-first. The circular icon button is reserved for submit inside the search bar. Ghost buttons are for secondary actions that live inside another component.

Lens chips

Raised white pills with a 17px line icon at 1.9 stroke. Exactly one chip is active at a time and it is the only Butter object on screen. The dashed chip is the expander and never takes the active state.

Search bar

The hero component. Largest radius on the page (28px), a soft blue-cast shadow, and a Blue border on focus. Placeholder text demonstrates real prompts. Nothing else on the page may have a stronger shadow.

Prompt cards

White card, hairline border, Butter-soft icon tile. Written in first person, always with a concrete constraint (budget, occasion, or preference). On hover the border disappears and the card lifts.

Voice

Do
  • Write as the user speaks: "Get me ready for a beach wedding."
  • Say what you give and what you get: "show us a product, we find a version that fits you better."
  • Sentence case everywhere except uppercase section labels.
  • Plain names on chips (Gifts, Swaps, Travel); Lens names in captions (Gift Lens, Swap Lens).
Do not
  • Use system language: catalog, API, query, GEO, deliverability.
  • Stack more than one helper line per control.
  • Promise outcomes in educational lenses. Routine and Fuel describe preferences, never treatment or health results.
  • Exceed two sentences for any helper text.
